# She Wasn't Alone

Status: pending
Proposed: 2026-10-09
Genre: Mystery / small-town whodunit → family drama
Setting: Port Ardoise, a fictional fishing town on Nova Scotia's South Shore, and Halifax (80 minutes
away). 2 February to 19 March 2027 (late winter). The death happened on Tue 12 January 2027.
Proposed parts: 20. This is long-form fair-play mystery with three layers: who has the phone, what
happened on 12 January, and who was there at six. Five suspects each need room for a real lie, an
explanation and an alibi, and the texter's identity has to be earned before the final answer. There
are four acts of five parts each: The Message (1–5), The Suspects (6–10), The Texter (11–15) and
Six O'Clock (16–20).

## Premise
Three weeks after Aunt June was found at the bottom of her cellar stairs, her missing phone texts the
family group chat: "she wasnt alone." June's niece, the executor of her estate, sets out to find out
who has the phone, why they're writing in June's name, and what really happened that night.

## Synopsis
June Pellerin, 71, a retired pharmacist, died on 12 January. The medical examiner ruled it an
accidental fall, and the case is closed. Her phone was never found. On 2 February the family chat
"Pellerins 🦞" lights up with a message from June's number. Tess, a court stenographer in Halifax who
writes down other people's words for a living, sets up Find My's "Notify When Found" on the family-shared
phone and starts building a timeline.

Everyone close to June turns out to have lied about the 12th. Tess's brother Owen was there that
afternoon asking for money. June's partner Glen left his curling bonspiel for three hours he won't
account for. A developer wanted the house. Then Tess finds June's unsent email to the College of
Pharmacists about missing narcotics at Harbour Drug, where June did relief shifts. That pulls in the
pharmacy tech whose initials are on the register, the delivery driver who saw June alive at 5:05, and
the warm, helpful owner, Paul Comeau.

Meanwhile the anonymous texter keeps surfacing in short bursts that move from the high school to the
wharf to a quiet residential street. They are scared, careful, and sixteen. When Tess finally learns
who is holding June's phone, she also learns why they couldn't just go to the police: they found it
in their father's truck.

## Main characters
- `tess` **Tess Pellerin** (34, device owner, court stenographer in Halifax, June's executor). She's
  precise and calm on the outside, and guilty that she cancelled the visit June asked for ("bring your
  sensible head"). Voice: full sentences, numbered lists, exact times ("At 4:52, not 5").
- `kofi` **Kofi Mensah** (36, Tess's husband, high-school chemistry teacher in Halifax). He's grounded
  and funny, and he's the one who notices the texter writes like his students and only pings at lunch
  and after the bell. Voice: dry jokes, "babe", voice-note references, short.
- `owen` **Owen Pellerin** (29, Tess's younger brother, runs a struggling landscaping business in
  Bridgewater). He's charming, defensive, ashamed. Voice: all lowercase, rapid bursts, 😭/🙄, "ok but".
- `glen` **Glen Ashby** (74, June's partner of six years, retired ferry engineer, still lives in her
  house). He's gentle, private, old-fashioned. Voice: ellipses everywhere... the occasional ACCIDENTAL
  CAPS, signs off "Glen".
- `margo` **Margo Doucette** (69, June's next-door neighbour and best friend, holds a spare key). She's
  observant and a gossip with good intentions. Voice: long paragraphs, "Now Tess,", "xo M".
- `leah` **Cst. Leah Morrison** (38, RCMP, Port Ardoise detachment). She's fair, overworked, and goes
  from polite dismissal to real engagement. Voice: brief, formal, no emoji.
- `paul` **Paul Comeau** (52, owner-pharmacist of Harbour Drug, June's former colleague, divorced). He's
  warm, generous, a pillar of the town, and the culprit. Voice: polished, exclamation marks, "Thinking
  of you all!", offers help a little too readily.
- `bree` **Bree Langille** (28, pharmacy technician at Harbour Drug). She's anxious and conscientious,
  and her initials were forged on the narcotic register. Voice: many quick messages, apologises
  constantly, "sorry sorry".
- `cody` **Cody Pike** (22, Harbour Drug delivery driver, works weekends on a boat). He's blunt and
  takes offence easily. Voice: "yo", one-line replies, no capitals.
- `kyle` **Kyle Brandt** (41, acquisitions agent, Harbourline Properties). He's slick and persistent,
  and a red herring. Voice: corporate, "Per my last", 👍.
- `noah` **Noah Comeau** (16, Paul's son, Grade 11, part-time wharf hand). June tutored him in
  chemistry. He is the anonymous texter. While texting from June's number, his voice is all lowercase,
  no apostrophes ("dont", "wasnt"), "ngl", and he never signs anything. June wrote in proper sentences
  and signed "J.", which is the first clue that it isn't her.
- **June Pellerin** (71, deceased) appears only in old messages Tess scrolls back to. The contact
  "Aunt June" is shared by her old messages and Noah's new ones, so the change in voice is visible on
  screen. (Implementation: a `june` character whose display name is "Aunt June". The continuity notes
  record which messages are June's and which are Noah's.)
- Automated senders: Find My, Harbour Drug SMS, Nova Scotia Medical Examiner Service (email), the
  Port Ardoise curling club chat, Bridgewater hospital parking.

## Relationships
Tess and Owen are siblings. Their parents died when Tess was 19, and June half-raised Owen. June was
the aunt to both. Glen is June's partner but not her husband, and the family half-accepts him (Owen
calls him "the boyfriend"). Tess and Kofi are married and have no children. Margo has been June's
neighbour for 40 years. Paul trained under June at Harbour Drug in the 1990s and bought the pharmacy
from its old owner. June did relief shifts there after retiring. Noah is Paul's son. His parents are
divorced and his mother lives in Bridgewater. June tutored Noah in chemistry for free on Thursdays.
Bree and Cody are Paul's staff.

## Central conflict
It's truth against loyalty, with everyone guarding a small secret that hides the big one. Tess has
to stop being the calm person who records what others say and start asking questions that hurt her
own family. Noah's version is harder: the truth about June means turning in his father.

## Secrets and planned twists
- **June's phone wasn't lost.** Holder: Paul (took it), then Noah (found it). Evidence: the RCMP list
  of personal effects has no phone (part 2), and Find My pings start after 31 Jan (part 2). Revealed
  piece by piece; Noah is confirmed as the holder in part 16.
- **Owen was at the house on the 12th** (2:00–2:40 pm), asking for $3,500 to stop his truck being
  repossessed. June said no. He told the family he hadn't seen her since Christmas. Holder: Owen, and
  the texter, who read June's messages. Evidence: the texter's "someone in that chat is lying about
  the 12th" (part 1), Margo seeing his truck (part 3). He admits it in part 4.
- **Glen left the bonspiel from 5:50 to 9:00 pm.** He was at the Bridgewater hospital getting the
  results of a scan (early-stage prostate cancer, very treatable). He and June had agreed to tell the
  family together. Evidence: a curling-club chat asking "where'd Glen go?" (part 5) and a hospital
  parking receipt SMS (part 6). He explains in part 6. This is a red herring that turns into tenderness.
- **Harbourline Properties wanted the house.** Kyle's emails say "following our conversation on
  January 12" (part 6). It was a phone call, and he spent 4–7 pm at a public council meeting (part 7).
  This one is a pure red herring.
- **June's unsent report.** A draft dated 11 Jan 11:52 pm in June's email (Tess has the password from
  June's notebook) is addressed to the Nova Scotia College of Pharmacists. It reports narcotic count
  discrepancies "on Tuesdays" at "a pharmacy where I provide relief coverage", with register entries
  initialled "B.L." Revealed at the end of part 8 and examined in part 9. This moves the story from
  family to town.
- **Bree's initials were forged.** Bree didn't work Tuesdays in December; she was at her nursing
  course. She kept her own count notebook because the numbers scared her. She's suspected in parts
  9–10 and cleared in part 12, after which she becomes an ally.
- **Cody saw June alive at 5:05.** He has a delivery signature. "fine at five" in the texter's message
  makes Tess suspect Cody is the texter (parts 10–11). He's cleared when a Find My ping happens in
  Port Ardoise while Cody is provably in Halifax at a hockey game (part 11).
- **The texter is Noah Comeau.** Fair-play clues: no apostrophes and lowercase (part 1 onward); pings
  only at school lunch, after the bell and on weekends (Kofi spots it in part 7); the high school, then
  the wharf, then Kaulbach Street (part 13, where Margo mentions "Paul's place on Kaulbach"); a "J."
  sign-off error when he tries to imitate June (part 12); and a reference to "thursdays" that only a
  tutee would know (part 14). He confirms it to Tess in part 16.
- **Paul is the culprit.** Fair-play clues: his condolence text says he last saw June "the week
  before" when she worked a relief shift for him on 11 Jan (part 5). He mentions the cellar "in the
  dark", and only Glen and the police knew the light was off (part 8). He keeps asking whether the
  police "traced that number" (parts 10 and 14). Paul does the narcotic orders on Tuesdays (Bree,
  part 12). Revealed in parts 16–19.
- **What happened at six (part 19, from the phone's contents and Paul's statement).** June texted
  Paul on 12 Jan at 4:48 pm: "I've written it up. I'll send it tomorrow unless you can explain." Paul:
  "Please let me come by. 6?" June: "Cody's just been. Come at 6, Glen's out." At the cellar door they
  argued over the box of photocopied register pages. June fell. Paul took the phone and the box, and
  left. A cancelled 911 call shows on the call log at 6:41 pm. The medical examiner's revised opinion
  is that she might have survived with prompt help. That's handled non-graphically, through formal
  emails and Leah's careful wording. Paul hid the phone in his truck's toolbox. Noah found it on
  31 Jan while borrowing the truck for a wharf job and opened it with June's birth year (visible on her
  lock-screen Medical ID).

## Planned ending
Exposure with a bittersweet resolution. Noah, supported by his mother, gives the phone to Leah with
Tess beside him. Paul is arrested and charged (criminal negligence causing death, drug diversion), and
the trial is pending, so the story doesn't invent a verdict. Owen tells the family chat the truth and
starts paying Glen's share of the house costs. Glen stays in the house, and his treatment begins. Bree
is cleared publicly by the College. In the final part the RCMP return June's phone to Tess as
executor. On it is one unsent draft to Tess from 12 Jan, 5:58 pm: "Tess, if anything about tomorrow
goes sideways, it's in the box in the cellar. Bring your sensible head. J." Tess's last message is
to Noah: "She'd have been proud of you. She said so every Thursday." Ending type: exposure /
bittersweet justice. There's no romance and no cheating plot.

## Part-by-part outline
### Act I — The Message
1. **She Wasn't Alone** (Tue 2 Feb). Hook: a lock-screen notification from "Pellerins 🦞": Aunt June:
   "she wasnt alone". Group chat: Owen's meme from earlier, then panic. Glen: "her phone was
   never found..." Tess calls and it goes to voicemail. Tess ↔ Kofi. Cliffhanger: a private text from
   Aunt June to Tess: "dont post in the group. someone in that chat is lying about the 12th".
2. **Notify When Found** (2–4 Feb). Tess ↔ Glen: June always kept the phone in her cardigan pocket.
   Tess ↔ Leah: the list of personal effects has no phone, the file is closed, "report the number to
   the carrier". Tess sets up Find My (shown in the notifications she gets). Owen is oddly hostile.
   Cliffhanger: Find My: "June's iPhone was found near Port Ardoise Regional High School. 12:14 PM".
3. **Not on the Phone** (4–6 Feb). Tess scrolls back through her own thread with June: 11 Jan, "Need
   to talk Saturday. Not on the phone. Bring your sensible head. J.", and Tess's "Can we do the
   23rd? Work is mad." Guilt. Margo's long message: June "out of sorts" that week, a truck in the
   lane on the 12th. Tess asks the texter "who are you?" Read, no reply. Cliffhanger: Margo: "Now
   Tess, it was Owen's truck. The green one. I didn't want to say."
4. **2:40** (6–7 Feb). Tess ↔ Owen: denial, deflection, then the admission across a long conversation
   with timestamps showing his delays. He asked for $3,500, June refused, he left angry at 2:40, she
   was alive. Kofi: "Do you believe him?" Cliffhanger: Aunt June: "he left at 2:40. she was fine at
   5". How could the texter know?
5. **Fine at Five** (7–9 Feb). Tess builds a timeline (a numbered-list text to Kofi). Glen says he was
   at the Chester bonspiel "all evening". Paul Comeau texts condolences and offers June's prescription
   records, mentioning he last saw her "the week before". The first Paul clue is planted. Cliffhanger:
   Glen forwards a photo of the bonspiel results, and in the curling-club group chat behind it is the
   line "anyone know where Glen took off to at 6??"

### Act II — The Suspects
6. **Three Hours** (9–11 Feb). Tess confronts Glen gently. Read receipts and a long silence. A
   Bridgewater hospital parking receipt SMS that Glen forwards by mistake while looking for something
   else. Glen explains the scan and the diagnosis they planned to tell the family together. Tess and
   Owen soften toward "the boyfriend". Cliffhanger: Tess logs into June's email (password from her
   notebook). An inbox screenshot shows Harbourline Properties: "Following our conversation on
   January 12…"
7. **Harbourline** (11–13 Feb). Kyle Brandt is pushy with the executor and evasive about the 12th.
   Owen wants to "go round there". Tess finds the public council-meeting minutes: Kyle presented
   4:00–7:10 pm. Kofi notices that every Find My ping falls at 12:10–12:50 or after 3:20, the school
   timetable, and says "Your texter is a kid." Cliffhanger: the Drafts folder count reads "Drafts (1)".
8. **Drafts (1)** (13–14 Feb). The draft's opening lines on screen: "To the Nova Scotia College of
   Pharmacists — I wish to report…" (11 Jan, 11:52 pm). Tess ↔ Kofi. Paul checks in warmly and says
   "those cellar stairs in the dark, I always worried". Tess doesn't catch it yet; the viewer can.
   Cliffhanger: the draft's last line: "Entries initialled B.L. on each Tuesday in question."
9. **Tuesdays** (14–16 Feb). Who is B.L.? Margo knows everyone: "Bree Langille, the tech, lovely
   girl." Tess asks the texter "did June tell you about the pharmacy?" Reply: "she told me to stay out
   of it". Leah agrees to "have a look" now that there's a document. Cliffhanger: Bree texts Tess
   first: "I know you're June's niece. Please don't go to the police about me before we talk."
10. **B.L.** (16–18 Feb). A long, anxious Bree thread. She swears she wasn't working Tuesdays in
    December. She sounds guilty, and won't say more until she's "sure". Paul tells Tess that Bree "has
    had a hard year, go easy" (pointing at her) and asks whether the police have "traced that number
    yet". Cliffhanger: the delivery log Paul sends to be helpful shows "Pellerin, J — 5:05 PM —
    signed — driver: C. Pike".

### Act III — The Texter
11. **Signed at 5:05** (18–20 Feb). Tess suspects Cody is the texter: he saw her "fine at five". Cody
    ↔ Tess: hostile and blunt ("yo im not ur texter"). Cody is in Halifax at a Mooseheads game,
    and his story about it is posted publicly. A Find My ping comes from Port Ardoise at the same
    minute. Cody is cleared, though he adds: "she was waiting on someone. had two mugs out".
    Cliffhanger: Aunt June to Tess: "stop asking cody. youre making it worse".
12. **Two Mugs** (20–23 Feb). The texter slips and signs a message "J.", the way June did, then: "sorry.
    that was dumb". Tess replies "You knew her well enough to copy her." Bree comes back with her
    count notebook. She was at her nursing practicum on December Tuesdays and has the university
    sign-in sheets. Paul places the narcotic orders on Tuesdays. Bree is cleared, and the suspicion
    shifts. Cliffhanger: Bree: "If it wasn't me, there's only one other person who signs that
    register."
13. **Kaulbach Street** (23–26 Feb). Find My pings on a weekend night on Kaulbach Street. Margo: "Now
    Tess, that's where Paul Comeau lives. Nice house. Him and the boy." Tess, shaken, tells Kofi and
    Leah. Leah: "Do not approach him." Paul texts, cheerful, inviting Tess to "come by the pharmacy,
    I've pulled June's files". Cliffhanger: Tess asks the texter "Do you live on Kaulbach Street?"
    and the typing indicator starts and stops three times.
14. **Thursdays** (26 Feb – 1 Mar). Tess doesn't go to the pharmacy and says so plainly to Leah. The
    texter breaks: "she helped me with chem on thursdays. she said i was good at it". Tess remembers
    June's Thursday tutoring of "the Comeau boy". Paul again asks whether "the number" was traced, and
    seems to know about the texts though nobody told him. Owen admits he told half the town at the
    pub. Cliffhanger: Aunt June: "i found it in my dads truck."
15. **The Toolbox** (1–3 Mar). Over a conversation that keeps stalling, Noah explains in pieces. On 31
    Jan he borrowed the truck, found the phone in the toolbox, opened it with her birth year (Medical
    ID), and read June's texts, including Owen's. He didn't understand the texts with his dad at
    first, then he did. He's terrified. Tess doesn't push. She brings in Kofi's advice on talking to
    a teenager, and suggests his mom. Cliffhanger: "he noticed its gone. hes asking me if i took
    anything from the truck".

### Act IV — Six O'Clock
16. **Noah** (3–4 Mar). The texter uses his name for the first time. Tess ↔ Leah (protecting Noah,
    getting him to a safe adult). Noah's mother, Shannon, texts Tess from Bridgewater. Paul to Tess:
    "Is my son talking to you?" Cliffhanger: Paul: "Whatever he thinks he saw, he's a kid. Call me
    before you do anything you can't take back."
17. **Chain of Custody** (4–5 Mar). Tess's job matters: she knows that evidence has to reach the
    police the right way. With Leah's guidance, Noah and Shannon hand the phone in at the Bridgewater
    detachment, with Tess present. Group chat: Tess finally tells the family everything, and Owen
    and Glen reconcile in text. Cliffhanger: Leah: "We've reopened the file. There is something on
    the phone you should hear from me, not the news."
18. **4:48 PM** (5–9 Mar). Leah walks Tess through what she can share. On screen are June's own
    messages to Paul from the 12th, shown as photos Leah lets Tess see in person and Tess relays to
    Kofi in her own words. The register photos are in June's camera roll. Paul's texts stop. Harbour
    Drug's automated SMS: "Pharmacy temporarily closed." Cliffhanger: Leah: "The call log shows a 911
    call from June's phone at 6:41 PM. It was cancelled after 0 seconds."
19. **Six O'Clock** (9–15 Mar). Paul is arrested. Charges are reported via a local news link. The
    medical examiner's amended-findings email reaches the executor, worded carefully and
    non-graphically. Tess, Owen and Glen grieve again, differently. Bree is cleared by the College.
    Noah, via Shannon: he wants to know if he did the right thing. Cliffhanger: Leah: "We're releasing
    June's phone to you as executor. There's an unsent message on it addressed to you."
20. **Bring Your Sensible Head** (19 Mar). The returned phone: June's unsent draft to Tess from 5:58
    pm on the 12th, about the box in the cellar. Tess sends it to the family chat. Owen's repayment
    plan, Glen's first treatment date, Margo's "xo M". Final exchange: Tess → Noah: "She'd have been
    proud of you. She said so every Thursday." Noah: "thanks. ngl i still have her chem notes" and a
    photo of June's neat handwriting. A quiet ending, not a twist.

## Continuity considerations
- Dates (2027): Mon 11 Jan, June's relief shift and the draft at 11:52 pm. Tue 12 Jan: Owen 2:00–2:40,
  Cody 5:05, Glen away 5:50–9:00, Paul around 6:00, the cancelled 911 call at 6:41, Glen finds her at
  9:10 pm. Paul takes the phone on 12 Jan. Noah finds it on 31 Jan. The first text is on Tue 2 Feb.
  Tess's Saturday visit was to be 16 Jan, and she postponed it to the 23rd.
- Find My pings must always fall at plausible times for Noah: 12:10–12:50 and 3:20+ on school days,
  any time at weekends. Never during the school day except at lunch.
- Noah's texts from June's number: lowercase, no apostrophes, never "J." except the deliberate slip in
  part 12. June's real messages: sentence case, full punctuation, "J."
- The cancelled 911 call and the medical examiner's revised opinion are presented factually and
  non-graphically. There is no on-screen violence. Paul's charges stay "charged / trial pending". Don't
  invent a verdict.
- Noah is a minor: nothing exploitative, a safe adult (Shannon) involved from part 16, and Leah
  handles him by the book.
- Fictional town and businesses (Port Ardoise, Harbour Drug, Harbourline Properties). Real
  institutions (RCMP, NS Medical Examiner Service, NS College of Pharmacists) are referenced only
  generically, with no invented officials quoted as real.
- Long monologues (Noah in part 15, Bree in part 10) are split into several short messages, per
  lessons-learned. Typing indicators only at the ends of screenshots.

## Originality assessment
Compared with 0001–0006 (there are no other pending outlines).
- **Genre:** the index's first mystery/whodunit. Every existing story is relationship drama or romance.
- **Premise:** a death investigation driven by an anonymous texter using a dead woman's phone. No
  existing story has a death, a crime, or the police.
- **Relationship setup:** an adult niece and an aunt (posthumously), siblings, an unmarried late-life
  partner, a neighbour, and a teenage witness. That combination is new. The sibling tension echoes
  0004's estrangement only loosely: here it's a lie about a visit, not an outing.
- **Secret / twist mechanism:** "Notify When Found" location pings, an unsent email draft and a
  narcotic register. It avoids the synced/shared iPad (0002, 0003), the misdirected text (0006), the
  wrong number (0004) and the landlord/rent trail (0001). Money appears only as Owen's red herring, not
  gambling (0003).
- **Twist:** the texter is the culprit's child, which reframes the anonymous messages as an act of
  conscience. That's distinct from 0002, where the child is the secret-holder but no one did wrong.
- **Ending:** exposure with bittersweet justice and a trial pending. The index has reconciliation,
  bittersweet family, breakup, hopeful, romance-with-cost and open endings, but no exposure of a crime.
- **Risks to watch:** 20 parts invites repetition. Each part must add one new fact or eliminate a
  suspect, with no filler "who are you?" exchanges. Paul's clues must be fair but quiet so the
  midpoint doesn't give him away.
