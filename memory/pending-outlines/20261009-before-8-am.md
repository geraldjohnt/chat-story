# Before 8 A.M.

Status: approved → story 0006
Proposed: 2026-10-09
Genre: Neighbours-feud romance / later-life love → open ending
Setting: Wellington, New Zealand; an apartment building in Mount Victoria; 2 March – 30 May 2027
(autumn)
Proposed parts: 3. Part 1 is the feud, part 2 is the thaw and the first lie found out, and part 3 is
the second secret and the choice. The story is intimate and small-scale, and more parts would dilute
it.

## Premise
A divorced piano teacher swaps passive-aggressive texts with the night-shift neighbour below her over a
noise complaint. They fall for each other. Then she finds out he filed the complaint after all, and
that he's leaving.

## Synopsis
The body corporate texts Ruth to say a noise complaint has been lodged about piano "before 8 am" from
unit 4B. She has one 7:15 student, a sixteen-year-old with exams. She gets the downstairs number from
the building directory and texts Iain, the new man in 3B, with ferocious politeness. He says he never
complained, but he does work nights at the 111 call centre and sleeps from 8. They negotiate: she moves
the early lesson, and he stops slamming the fire door at 7:40. The texts go from formal to funny to
something else. He leaves a note on her door: "The Chopin on Tuesday was good." Then the body
corporate's email gives the complaint's date and the unit that lodged it: 3B, 14 February, his first
week. After she forgives that, a misdirected text meant for his daughter lands in Ruth's thread: he's
moving to Dunedin on 30 May.

## Main characters
- `ruth` **Ruth Danvers** (48, device owner, piano teacher who teaches from home, divorced four years).
  Her ex left with three weeks' notice. She's wry, self-sufficient, wary of being blindsided. Voice:
  complete sentences, semicolons, signs early texts "Ruth (4B)", warms into ellipses and the odd 🙂.
- `iain` **Iain Mackie** (52, 111 emergency call-taker, Glasgow-born, widowed six years). He's kind,
  conflict-avoidant, embarrassed by small lies. Voice: dry Scottish phrasing ("aye", "wee"), short
  texts, full stops, sends song titles instead of feelings.
- `sam` **Sam Danvers** (22, Ruth's son, uni student in Auckland). He teases, protects, and finds his
  mum's love life both hilarious and touching. Voice: "MUM.", 😭, memes described in words, "proud
  of u".
- `kerry` **Kerry Walsh** (body corporate manager, Harbourview Body Corporate). Brisk, polite,
  boilerplate.
- `fiona` **Fiona Mackie** (26, Iain's daughter, Dunedin, pregnant with twins). She appears only
  through the misdirected text and a late message. Voice: warm, practical.

## Relationships
Ruth and Iain are strangers, then adversaries, then something tender. Ruth and Sam are a close mother
and son, and he's her confidant. Iain and Fiona are a father and daughter who are close. Iain's wife
died six years ago. Ruth owns 4B. Iain rents 3B on a six-month fixed term (so there's no landlord plot:
the body corporate handles noise rules).

## Central conflict
Ruth wants a second love that doesn't blindside her the way her marriage ended. Iain is a decent man
whose small evasions add up to the exact thing she fears. The question is whether two honest-ish adults in
their late forties and fifties can risk something with an end date.

## Secrets and planned twists
- Iain filed the first complaint (on 14 Feb, his first week, before he knew her), then denied it.
  Evidence: Kerry's email with the date and the lodging unit (part 1 cliffhanger). He admits it in
  part 2: "I was knackered and you were a stranger. Then you weren't."
- Iain applied in January to transfer to the Dunedin comms centre to be near Fiona's twins. Evidence:
  the misdirected text "Told the agent I'll be out by 30 May, transfer's confirmed x" (part 2
  cliffhanger). Explained in part 3.
- Ruth's ex left with three weeks' notice. She mentions it lightly in part 1 and it lands in part 3,
  so her reaction is motivated, not melodrama.

## Planned ending
Open and hopeful, with no promise. Ruth says she won't do long-distance at 48 on six weeks' evidence,
and then they spend the six weeks anyway, eyes open. Iain moves on 30 May. Final screenshots: on
30 May at 7:52 am Ruth sends a photo of Chopin sheet music: "Played it before 8. Nobody complained."
Iain: "Dunedin has pianos. Just saying." Ruth: "…Saying what, exactly?" and the typing indicator ends
the story. Ending type: ambiguous/open (the index doesn't use this one yet).

## Part-by-part outline
1. **Noise Complaint** (2–20 Mar). Lock-screen text from Harbourview: "complaint re: noise, unit 4B".
   Ruth ↔ Iain: an icy exchange, signed off with unit numbers. Ruth ↔ Sam: "MUM. are you feuding."
   They negotiate a truce, then a timestamp-spanning thaw: song titles, the Chopin note, a shared
   complaint about the lift. Ruth asks Kerry for the complaint record "for my files". Cliffhanger:
   Kerry's email shows "Lodged 14/02/2027, Unit 3B".
2. **3B** (20 Mar – 18 Apr). Ruth ↔ Iain: "You've never complained about anything in your life, you
   said." His awkward, honest admission. She lets him sweat for a day. Read receipt, no reply, then she
   forgives him with terms ("You'll carry my keyboard to the school concert"). The first date happens
   offscreen, on Oriental Bay. Sam: "proud of u. also gross." Warm weeks pass in a Notification Center
   of good-morning texts after his shifts. Cliffhanger: a misdirected text in their thread: "Told the
   agent I'll be out by 30 May, transfer's confirmed x". Seconds later: "That wasn't meant for you."
3. **Thirtieth of May** (18 Apr – 30 May). Iain explains: the January application, Fiona's twins due in
   June, and that he didn't think he'd meet anyone. Ruth ↔ Sam: the ex who left with three weeks'
   notice. Ruth tells Iain she won't do long-distance. Iain doesn't argue, and asks for the six weeks.
   A short line from Fiona (she got Ruth's number from her dad): "He talks about you. Sorry for the
   wrong-number mess." A conversation list on moving day. The final exchange is the Chopin message
   and the typing indicator.

## Originality assessment
Compared with 0001, 0002, 0003, and this batch's "Is This Still Ben?" and "Split the Rental".
- It's the only later-life romance (48 and 52) and the only neighbour setup.
- The secrets are small, human evasions, not large deceptions.
- The misdirected-text device is new to the index.
- The body corporate replaces a landlord to avoid echoing 0001.
- The ending (open, ending on a typing indicator) is the first ambiguous ending in the index. The
  typing indicator is the final item, which is valid per lessons-learned.
- It differs from "Split the Rental": no workplace and no professional stakes. The obstacle is
  geography and old hurt.
