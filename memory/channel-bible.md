# Channel bible

## Identity and tone
A storytelling channel of **fictional** relationship dramas told through text-message screenshots.
Tone: emotionally sharp, grounded, a little suspenseful — never cruel for its own sake. Viewers should feel
they are piecing together the truth alongside the device owner. Stories are clearly fiction; never frame
them as real, leaked or "found" conversations, and never use real people.

## English writing standards
- Natural contemporary English; contractions; varied sentence length. Typos only when they reveal
  character (rushed, drunk, panicking) — sparingly.
- Each character has a distinct texting voice (punctuation, capitalisation, emoji use, message length,
  response speed). Keep a voice consistent across parts.
- Exposition must be something a person would actually text. No "As you know, we've been married ten years".

## Relationship-drama conventions
- Suspicion should be supported or challenged by **evidence** visible in the screenshots (a notification,
  a timestamp, an attachment, a slip in wording).
- Reveals have consequences; escalation is earned; characters act from motive, not plot convenience.
- Cliffhangers land on a genuine turn (new information, a decision, an unanswered question), not on
  arbitrary cut-offs. A complete story resolves its major threads.

## Pacing and format
- Plan screenshots around beats: one beat ≈ one screenshot group. Mix types (conversation, lock-screen
  notification, Notification Center, inbox) when it serves the story.
- 6–25 messages per conversation screenshot is typical; the renderer splits overflow automatically.
- Use timestamps to show time passing; read receipts and typing indicators to build tension — sparingly.
- Part count follows the story's needs (often 2–6), not a fixed formula.

## Audience expectations
Short-form vertical video viewers: fast hook in the first screenshot, clear stakes by the end of part 1,
readable text (never shrink content to fit), satisfying payoff.

## Originality rules
- Before outlining, compare with `story-index.json` and pending outlines: premise, relationship setup,
  secret, twist mechanism, and ending type must not substantially repeat an existing story.
- Vary settings, ages, relationship types (partners, siblings, roommates, friends, co-parents,
  in-laws, colleagues), and ending types (reconciliation, breakup, exposure, bittersweet, ambiguous).
- Avoid formula: not every story needs a cheating reveal or a last-message twist.
